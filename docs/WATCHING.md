## `venus_phases.mp4` — 2:30

The film answers three questions, in order, and every number on screen is live
from the ephemeris rather than a caption.

| At | Shot | What it is establishing |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | THE INNER SOLAR SYSTEM | Wide. Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars on their real orbits. |
| 0:12 | VENUS ORBITS INSIDE US | The one fact everything else follows from: Venus is closer to the Sun than we are. |
| 0:28 | TWO ORBITS, ONE LIGHT | The Sun is the only light source in the scene. |
| 0:45 | FROM ECLIPTIC NORTH | A dolly-zoom — the camera pulls from 2.5 AU to 15 AU while the field narrows 41°→11.5°, so the framed extent barely changes and perspective flattens into a diagram. The move *is* the transition from picture to schematic. |
| 1:00 | THE ANGLE CHANGES | **The core shot.** Held top-down while ten months run. The Sun–Venus–Earth triangle, the elongation arc at Earth, the phase angle at Venus, and the two tangent lines from Earth to Venus' orbit — that cone is *why Venus never leaves the Sun*, and its half-angle is the greatest elongation. |
| 1:25 | WHAT THE EARTH SEES | From beside the Earth. Venus is a point of light, because that is what it is. |
| 1:35 | SUPERIOR CONJUNCTION | Split screen: geometry left, telescope right. Venus beyond the Sun — full, and tiny. Then a hard cut to the telescope alone. |
| 1:47 | GREATEST EASTERN ELONGATION | Same split. The line of sight runs tangent to Venus' orbit, so we see it half lit. |
| 1:59 | TOWARDS INFERIOR CONJUNCTION | Venus swings between us and the Sun. The crescent thins as the disc swells. |
| 2:11 | SAME SCALE, SAME FIELD | **The payoff.** All three moments side by side at one fixed angular scale. 9.8″ full → 24.3″ half → 59.8″ crescent, with distances 1.71 → 0.69 → 0.28 AU captioned underneath. |
| 2:21 | WHY IT NEVER LEAVES THE SUN | Earth's limb below, Venus and the Sun in one frame, 24° apart — and never much more. |

**What to check while watching.** The HUD carries elongation, phase angle,
illuminated fraction, apparent diameter, magnitude and distance, every frame,
straight from the ephemeris. Two captions are standing disclosures: the
exaggeration factor whenever bodies are inflated, and `FIXED 0.13"/PIXEL - NO
ZOOM` on every telescopic view. An asterisk appears on the magnitude when Venus
passes outside the fitted range of Hilton's photometric law.

**The claim the film has to earn** is that a crescent Venus looks *bigger*
because it is nearer. That is only meaningful if the scale is trustworthy, which
is why the second half never changes its angular scale and says so on screen.

## `september2026.mp4` — 30 s

The companion: the Moon passing in front of Venus on 2026 September 14, seen
from Chennai. The clock is deliberately non-linear — the encounter spans hours
but the contacts last seconds, so each segment carries its own easing.

`THE DAY OF` → `CLOSING IN` (decelerating into first contact, zooming 11°→1.5°)
→ `DISAPPEARANCE` (near real time) → `HIDDEN BEHIND THE MOON` → `REAPPEARANCE`
→ `THE MOON PULLS AWAY`.

The occultation is not scripted. Venus disappears because the Moon's sphere is
nearer to the observer than Venus' sphere and gets in the way — the same reason
it happens in the sky. Watch the Moon's dark limb: it is *absent* in daylight,
because the renderer adds the full airlight in front of it, and appears once the
sky darkens.
